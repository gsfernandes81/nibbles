Design proposal · 2026-10-10 · for review before any code

ytq and dlq, buttons first

The second draft. The first kept today's screens and added buttons to them. This one reshapes both apps around a phone: one shell with tabs along the bottom, sheets that rise from the bottom instead of pages, and two taps from a search result to a queued video. Every mockup is drawn with bewitch's own recipes at the real terminal size.

The shape

One app, five tabs

Search, Feed, Later, Queue and More, along the bottom where the thumb is. ytq opens on Search, ytq subs on Feed and dlq on Queue. dlq's listing already ran inside ytq's session; now it is simply a tab.

Sheets, not pages

A video, a download, a confirm and a setting each open as a sheet from the bottom over the screen they came from, which stays visible and dimmed above it. Esc, or a tap on the dimmed part, closes the sheet.

Two taps to queue

Tap a result and its sheet opens at once while the formats load. Tap Queue. Quality is a tier (a height, or an audio bitrate) plus Smallest or Largest, and the full table is a button away, opened at that tier.

Keyboard up or down

Termux shrinks the terminal when the phone keyboard opens, so every screen is laid out by how many rows it gets, shown below side by side. Below 16 rows: the window card becomes one line, and a sheet takes the full height with its main button pinned and the rest scrolling. Tabs and buttons never shrink. The 24 and 14 rows here are estimates, to be measured on your phone.

Your clock, not UTC

The window is named by when it opens on the phone's own clock: “at 03:00”, “tomorrow 03:00”, “today 14:00”, whichever 00:00 UTC minus the window turns into where you are. The reset reads the same way (“before the 04:00 reset”). There is no UTC and no “tonight” on any screen, and nothing is called free or paid unless it really is. The mockups assume a phone at UTC+4.

No Back buttons

Tabs replace the stack of screens. The only depth left is a sheet, and a sheet closes. Quit sits in More, and q still quits from anywhere.

How touch works

A tap is a tap, a drag is a scroll

Termux decides this before the app hears anything (checked in its TerminalView source). A tap reaches the app as press and release together, the moment the finger lifts, and only if the finger did not move. A drag never sends a press; it arrives as wheel events, one per row moved, followed by a fling's momentum. So a drag can never press a button, and a button acts as soon as Termux reports the tap. It fills with the primary colour for one frame so you see what you hit. A long press is Termux's own text selection and never reaches the app.

A short guard against double taps

For 100 ms after a screen or dialog appears, taps are ignored, which is under the ~100 ms at which a response starts to feel slow. For a further 200 ms (Android's own double-tap window is 300 ms in total), only a tap on the same cell as the one that opened the screen is dropped. A deliberate tap anywhere else acts at once, so the second half of a double tap cannot land on a dialog's yes.

One tap opens

Tapping a row opens its sheet in the same tap. In ytq that also starts the format fetch (~0.3 MB), and the sheet fills in when it lands. The selection bar still shows which row the keys act on.

The price is on the button

A button that spends data names the amount on its face, or on the line directly above it. Anything that spends data nothing is counting (mobile data, with the portal out of reach) still goes through exactly one yes.

Swiping scrolls, and a deliberate pull can spend

Termux turns a swipe into wheel events. A swipe scrolls the list. On Feed and Later, the end of the list carries the next page's total price; once the list has rested there, a fresh drag down reads it. A fling's momentum stops at the end and never fetches, because flicks on Termux are not reliable enough to spend on.

Draggable scrollbars

The last three columns of a list are the scrollbar's zone. A tap there jumps to that point. Termux stamps every wheel event of a drag with the cell where the drag began, so a drag that starts on the bar moves the thumb a row per row of finger travel, a page at a time through the list, while a drag anywhere else scrolls the list a row at a time.

Fetches run in the background

Search, format probes and the deeper look run on worker threads. The screen stays live, a spinner shows what is running, and Stop (x) cancels it.

Keys still work

Every key still works. Each button shows its key in grey, so the button row is the hint line. The two hint sets become two label sets: full words at 38 columns and up, short ones at 30.

Thumb zone and keyboard

Tabs, sheets and their buttons sit at the bottom. Anything you type into (the search bar, a setting, a rename) sits at the top, above the phone keyboard.

An idle screen is still silent

Everything is drawn through bewitch.screen.Frame. With nothing running, the loop blocks on inkey() and writes nothing. Only spinners, the selected title's marquee and a running download animate.

The pieces

Finding and queueing (ytq's half)

write_item is still the one door, and duplicates are still refused on the extractor and id.

The queue (dlq's half)

Every button calls the same function as its key and its command (do_arm, do_cancel, set_dest, queue_run_argv, do_reorder). The cut line, gate() and admit() stay exactly as they are.

Already settled in your comments: a tap acts as soon as Termux reports it, a 100 ms guard plus a same-spot double-tap guard, one tap opens a row, one tap spends when the price is showing, and uncounted data needs exactly one yes. Buttons stay three rows tall (about 11 mm) at every height; lists give up rows first.
